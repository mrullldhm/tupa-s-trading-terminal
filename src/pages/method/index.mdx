## The goal

Get the same numbers TradingView's Strategy Tester would give on **CL1!**, front-month crude oil futures, on
1-minute bars. Same fills, same costs, same report. The difference: it runs hundreds of settings in one go, and
every number can be checked.

## Fixed settings

These match the Properties tab of a TradingView strategy, and they never change between strategies.

<FixedRules />

Commission is a fee per order. Slippage is a worse fill price. They are separate costs and shown separately.
Margin is not checked, so "margin calls: 0" in a report only means margin was switched off.

## How orders fill

- **Signals act on the next candle.** A strategy looks at each candle when it closes. Any order it places fills
  from the next candle, like TradingView's default.
- **The path inside a candle.** A 1-minute candle only shows open, high, low and close. Like TradingView, the
  tester assumes price went open → the nearer extreme → the other extreme → close. Stops and limits fill in that
  order.
- **Gaps.** If a candle opens past a stop or limit, it fills at the open.
- **Slippage.** Market and stop orders fill N ticks worse. Limit orders, like a profit target, never slip.
- **One position at a time.** No adding to a trade.

## Sessions

CL trades almost 24 hours, so "Asia", "London" and "New York" are not official. Each session open is the minute
where volume jumps, measured on two years of CL data: 09:00 UTC+8 for Asia, 08:00 London, and 09:00 New York.
Daylight saving time is handled for each city.

## One rule that is mine, not TradingView's

<Callout title="Roll exit (owner rule)">
  <p>A futures contract expires, and CL1! switches to the next contract. If a trade is still open on the last bar of
  a contract, the tester closes it there. TradingView would hold it across the switch and count the price gap
  between contracts as profit or loss. In practice this affects 0 or 1 trades per session.</p>
</Callout>

## What "matches TradingView" can and cannot mean

The tester uses Databento's front-month data. TradingView's CL1! also switches contract by volume, but with its own
rule, so a few roll days can differ by about a day, and prices can differ slightly between data vendors. One known
case: on 20 April 2020, when May crude went to −$37.63, Databento had already switched to June.

So the only real proof is a TradingView export of the same strategy. That is why every strategy also has a Pine
twin: the same rules written for TradingView, so trades can be compared on the real chart.

## How results are checked

- **Tests.** The engine has a test suite for fills, costs and edge cases.
- **Independent review.** Codex reviews the code before results count.
- **Independent replication.** For headline results, Codex writes its own engine, without using any of my code,
  and rebuilds the result from the same data. If every trade matches, the result counts as replicated.
