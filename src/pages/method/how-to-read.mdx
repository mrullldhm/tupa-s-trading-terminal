## The three stages

Every strategy goes through the same three stages, in order. A tick on a stage means it is done, not that the
strategy passed it.

- **F1, test every setting.** Every combination of settings runs over the full history. The best one at 0 ticks
  is shown, then rerun at 1 and 2 ticks of slippage.
- **F2, narrow search.** A guess is written down first, then searched on data before 2024 only, scored at 1 tick.
  Every run is logged, including the failures.
- **F3, validate on unseen data.** Walk-forward tests and the reserved 2024+ data. Not built yet.

## In-sample

**The settings were picked on the same data they are reported on.** Picking the best of many tries on known data
always looks better than what the same rules will earn on new data. Treat an in-sample result as an upper bound,
not a forecast.

## Best of N

"Best of 192" means 192 settings were tried and only the best is shown. The bigger N is, the more likely the winner
is just lucky. F2 pages show both the size of that run and the total number of tries across all runs, since
every try counts against the result.

## Reserved is not clean out-of-sample

Data from 1 January 2024 on is **reserved**: F2 searches never use it. But the starting settings came from
F1, which used all the data, and an early check also touched 2024+. So reserved data is less contaminated,
not untouched. Each strategy's Evidence page lists the known exceptions.

## Slippage scenarios

- **0 ticks** still pays commission: $4.02 per round trip is in every number.
- **1 tick and 2 ticks** are separate test runs, not a simple subtraction. Trade counts can differ slightly
  between them, so each scenario shows its own count.
- Only market and stop orders slip. A trade that exits at its limit target slips once, not twice.

## Net profit and the search objective

**Net profit** is the sum of all closed trades. The F2 search scored settings on net profit at 1 tick
**plus** the value of any trade still open at the end. Usually there is none and the two are the same; when they
differ, both are shown.

## The P&L charts

The charts show **cumulative closed-trade profit, sampled at the end of each month**. They do not show swings
inside a month or open trades. Do not read drawdown off the chart.

## Year, month, week and day tables

These show net profit at 0, 1 and 2 ticks, grouped by the date each trade **closed**, in UTC. UTC days are not
CL trading days: a trade that closes at 9 pm New York time already counts on the next UTC day, and a week runs
Monday to Sunday. Most days hold one trade, so a day row is close to a single trade's result. Like everything else, these
tables are in-sample.

## Drawdown

**Max drawdown** is the biggest drop from a high point in equity, measured inside each candle while a trade is open,
like TradingView. The percentage is of the $50,000 starting capital.

It can go **above 100%**. The test always trades 1 contract, whatever the account balance, and it never stops
for a margin call. A drawdown above 100% means the drop from a high point was bigger than the whole $50,000
starting account.

## Sharpe and Sortino

Both use monthly returns and are **not annualized**, as TradingView reports them. Do not compare them with yearly
Sharpe ratios you see elsewhere.

## Replicated

A replication badge means Codex rebuilt that exact result with its own engine and every trade matched. It covers
only the sessions, features and slippage levels named on the badge. It is not a TradingView export.
