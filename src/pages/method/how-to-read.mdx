## The research stages

Every strategy goes through the same five stages, in order. A tick on a stage means it is done, not that the
strategy passed it.

- **F1, test every setting.** Every combination of settings runs over the full history. The best one
  is shown after commission.
- **F2, narrow search.** A guess is written down first, then searched on data before 2024 only. Saved searches need re-evaluation after the limit-entry updates.
  Every run is logged, including the failures.
- **F3, stress the search.** Still on data before 2024. Re-run the F2 search each January using only the years
  before it, trade its pick for that year, and add up the years (walk-forward). Then check how often a search
  winner falls apart on the other half of the data (PBO), and whether the result beats the best of all the tries
  by luck (deflated Sharpe ratio, DSR). A result goes on only if it passes rules written down before the run.
- **F4, prop challenge.** Planned. Would the result pass a Topstep 50K evaluation, and is that better than random
  entries under the same rules?
- **F5, final check on reserved data.** Planned. One run on 2024+ data, used once.

## In-sample

**The settings were picked on the same data they are reported on.** Picking the best of many tries on known data
always looks better than what the same rules will earn on new data. Treat an in-sample result as an upper bound,
not a forecast.

## Best of N

"Best of 192" means 192 settings were tried and only the best is shown. The bigger N is, the more likely the winner
is just lucky. F2 pages show both the size of that run and the total number of tries across all runs, since
every try counts against the result.

## Reserved is not clean out-of-sample

Data from 1 January 2024 on is **reserved**: F2 searches never use it. But the starting settings came from
F1, which used all the data, and an early check also touched 2024+. So reserved data is less contaminated,
not untouched. Each strategy's Evidence page lists the known exceptions.

## Execution assumptions and commission

The site displays the existing baseline results, with $4.02 commission per round trip and no added fill-price
adjustment. The planned update is to use limit buy/sell entries at the specified price when filled. Current
results still use the saved strategy rules; the limit-entry versions have not been tested yet.

## Net profit and the search objective

**Net profit** is the sum of all closed trades after commission. Open P&L is the value of any trade still open
at the end, shown separately. The saved F2 searches used previous execution assumptions for selection. Their
historical verdicts are not a fresh ranking of the displayed baseline results.

## The P&L charts

The charts show **cumulative closed-trade profit, sampled at the end of each Malaysian calendar month**. They do not show swings
inside a month or open trades. Do not read drawdown off the chart.

## Daily results and trade charts

Daily results group net profit after commission by the Malaysian date each trade **closed**, latest first.
All chart times and execution records use **Malaysia time (MYT, UTC+8)**. A trade that closes at 18:30 UTC
appears at 02:30 MYT on the following day. Malaysia does not change clocks for daylight saving.

The local trade chart includes the whole selected trade, even when its entry was on an earlier day.
These are in-sample backtest fills, not live executions.

## Drawdown

**Max drawdown** is the biggest drop from a high point in equity, measured inside each candle while a trade is open,
like TradingView. The percentage is of the $50,000 starting capital.

It can go **above 100%**. The test always trades 1 contract, whatever the account balance, and it never stops
for a margin call. A drawdown above 100% means the drop from a high point was bigger than the whole $50,000
starting account.

## Sharpe and Sortino

Both use monthly returns and are **not annualized**, as TradingView reports them. Do not compare them with yearly
Sharpe ratios you see elsewhere.

## Replicated

A replication badge means Codex rebuilt the result named on the badge with its own code. For F1 and F2 that is
its own engine, and every trade matched. For F3 it is the yearly picks and scores recomputed from the saved
results, plus the test years named on the badge re-run with its own engine. A badge covers only what it names.
It is not a TradingView export.
