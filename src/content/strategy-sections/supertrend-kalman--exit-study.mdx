## Exit study: breakeven and trailing stop

Here the entry and the first stop stay exactly as F1 found them, and only the exit changes. **T** is the target
distance: how far the profit target sits from the entry. The study tried breakeven by touch, by close and by time;
trailing stops that follow a fraction of T, an ATR line, the last swing pivot, a step ratchet, the best close, an
EMA or Parabolic SAR; combinations with three declared breakevens; and a plain time exit. Stops move at each
1-minute close and work from the next bar.

### What this means

Exit rules do not change the picture. In All hours and New York, the two biggest variants, no rule really beat the
fixed stop and target. All hours: 0 of 427. New York: the best gained $40 over 16 years. In All hours, every
breakeven and trailing family cut net profit sharply, and some turned it negative. Asia and London improved
slightly. The best London rule (breakeven at half the target plus a 1-minute EMA(9) trail, target removed) added
$2,440 after commission over 16 years. Those gains are the best of
428 tries on the same data. They are about the size that picking the best of many rules can produce by luck. They
were found on the full history, including the reserved 2024+ years, so they cannot be checked there.
