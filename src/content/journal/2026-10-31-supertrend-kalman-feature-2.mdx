The F1 test (every setting) tried 6,912 settings of this trend idea and none survived 2 ticks of slippage. The F2 search asked one
question: can a filter, known at the moment of entry, make it survive?

This time I wrote the pass rules down **before** the search, with Codex reviewing them first. A result had to
make money at 1 and 2 ticks, two thirds of its nearby settings had to make money too, and no single year could
supply more than half of the profit. The search used data before 2024 only. It tried 2,000 settings across the
four versions.

The biggest result failed. In the all-hours version, skipping every trade with a stop under 100 ticks went from
−$34,756 to +$109,195 at 2 ticks. That is the cost idea I wrote down in advance, so it was tempting. But 2022
alone made 53.5% of the profit, and 2017 had five trades. A 100-tick stop mostly happens when oil is very
volatile, so the filter is a bet on volatile years. The rule said no, so it is no. The guided search then found
an even bigger one (+$90,347 at 2 ticks), and 93% of that was 2022.

What is left is small. In New York the baseline already made money before 2024. The winning filter only raises the
baseline's own RVI threshold from 50 to 55: +$60,457 at 1 tick and +$16,487 at 2 ticks, before 2024. Codex rebuilt
it with its own code and every one of the 2,247 trades matched.

It is in-sample and the best of many tries. Next is F3, validation on unseen data: walk-forward testing and the reserved 2024+ data.
