The F1 test (every setting) said the problem was costs. So before searching, I wrote down one main guess: trades with a small stop
lose the most at 1 tick, because $20 of slippage is a big share of a small stop.

The search used only data before 2024 and scored every setting on its profit **at 1 tick**. It tried 1,500
settings across the three sessions. Six runs, every one listed on the [strategy page](/strategies/fair-price-reversal/#search-for-a-better-filter-f2) with its verdict.

The guess held. In New York and London, the winner was the same simple rule: skip trades whose stop is smaller
than about 30 ticks. Nearby values also worked. London went from −$13,754 to +$32,197 at 1 tick on the development
data. New York went from +$2,423 to +$49,297. Codex rebuilt the New York result independently and every trade
matched.

Why it is not an edge yet:
- It was picked on the same data it is reported on, from 1,500 tries.
- The starting settings came from F1, which saw all data up to 2026. An early check also touched 2024+.
- 58% of London's profit came from 2022, a very volatile year.

Next is F3, validation on unseen data: walk-forward testing and the reserved 2024+ data.
