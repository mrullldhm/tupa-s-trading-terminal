summary: Re-running each F2 search year by year, and asking whether its winner beats the best of 200 lucky tries. Every candidate failed the luck check.

F2 left three candidates: a minimum-stop filter for Fair Price Reversal in New York and in London, and a small
RVI filter for Supertrend + Kalman in New York. All three were picked on data before 2024. F3 asks whether they
survive the way they were found, still without touching 2024+.

Three checks, with the rules for passing written down before any run and reviewed by Codex:

- **Walk-forward.** Each January from 2014 to 2023, re-run the F2 search using only the years before it, trade its
  pick for that year, then add the years up. The total has to make money at 1 and at 2 ticks of slippage, and at
  least 7 of the 10 years have to make money at 1 tick.
- **PBO.** Split the data into two halves 12,870 ways. How often does the best setting of one half land in the
  bottom half of the other? At most half the time.
- **Deflated Sharpe ratio.** The best of 200 random tries looks good even if none of them has an edge. The
  candidate's Sharpe ratio has to clear that luck bar with 95% confidence.

**None of the three cleared the luck bar.** The New York Fair Price Reversal filter made money walk-forward, but
in only 6 of 10 years, and 2023 carried most of it. London's search lost money. The Supertrend New York candidate
passed the walk-forward and PBO checks, with exactly the 7 profitable years at 1 tick the rule needs, but its Sharpe ratio sits below what the
luckiest of 200 tries reaches.

Codex recomputed every yearly pick, the PBO and the deflated Sharpe from the saved results with its own code,
and re-ran one test year (Supertrend, 2015) with its own engine. Everything it checked matched.

So F4, the Topstep 50K prop challenge, has nothing to test yet. That is the point of putting F3 first: a prop
layer cannot create an edge, it can only shape risk around one. The next idea starts again at F1.
