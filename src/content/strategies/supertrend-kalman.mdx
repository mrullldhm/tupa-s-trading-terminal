## The idea

A trend-following idea: Supertrend and an ATR trailing stop (the "UT Bot" line), read on **15-minute candles**
built from the 1-minute data. A Kalman filter estimates the trend's slope, and a trade is only taken in the
direction of that slope. A volatility filter avoids trades whose stop would be too wide. The stop goes at the last
swing high or low, and the target is 2 or 3 times the risk.

I left every design choice open, so each one became a setting to test: which signal starts a trade, which
volatility filter, and how the trade exits. Click through the steps below to see one trade from start to finish.

## The rules

These are the rules behind the saved results. Entries will be updated to limit buy/sell at the planned price;
that strategy change has not been tested yet.

- **15-minute candles.** Built from the 1-minute bars. A candle's values are used only once it is complete: the
  decision is made on the first 1-minute bar of the next candle, and the order fills one minute later.
- **Entry signal** (a setting): Supertrend flips to the trade side; or price crosses the ATR trail while
  Supertrend agrees; or both agree for the first time. In every case the Kalman slope must point the same way.
- **Stop.** The last confirmed 15-minute swing low (for a long) or swing high (for a short).
- **Target.** 2R or 3R, where R is the distance from the decision close to the stop.
- **Volatility filter** (a setting): none; skip trades whose stop is wider than a multiple of ATR; skip when
  short-term ATR is high against long-term ATR; or require RVI on the trade side.
- **Exit** (a setting): fixed stop and target; the stop trails the ATR line; or close the trade when Supertrend
  flips against it.
- **Hours.** Four versions: all hours, or only inside the Asia, London or New York session (closed at the session
  end).

Same fixed properties as every test: $50,000, 1 contract, $2.01 commission per order, results after commission. [How the tester works](/method/).

## Exit study: breakeven and trailing stop

Here the entry and the first stop stay exactly as F1 found them, and only the exit changes. **T** is the target
distance: how far the profit target sits from the entry. The study tried breakeven by touch, by close and by time;
trailing stops that follow a fraction of T, an ATR line, the last swing pivot, a step ratchet, the best close, an
EMA or Parabolic SAR; combinations with three declared breakevens; and a plain time exit. Stops move at each
1-minute close and work from the next bar.

### What this means

Exit rules do not change the picture. In All hours and New York, the two biggest variants, no rule really beat the
fixed stop and target. All hours: 0 of 427. New York: the best gained $40 over 16 years. In All hours, every
breakeven and trailing family cut net profit sharply, and some turned it negative. Asia and London improved
slightly. The best London rule (breakeven at half the target plus a 1-minute EMA(9) trail, target removed) added
$2,440 after commission over 16 years. Those gains are the best of
428 tries on the same data. They are about the size that picking the best of many rules can produce by luck. They
were found on the full history, including the reserved 2024+ years, so they cannot be checked there.
