## The idea

A trend-following idea: Supertrend and an ATR trailing stop (the "UT Bot" line), read on **15-minute candles**
built from the 1-minute data. A Kalman filter estimates the trend's slope, and a trade is only taken in the
direction of that slope. A volatility filter avoids trades whose stop would be too wide. The stop goes at the last
swing high or low, and the target is 2 or 3 times the risk.

I left every design choice open, so each one became a setting to test: which signal starts a trade, which
volatility filter, and how the trade exits. Click through the steps below to see one trade from start to finish.

## The rules

- **15-minute candles.** Built from the 1-minute bars. A candle's values are used only once it is complete: the
  decision is made on the first 1-minute bar of the next candle, and the order fills one minute later.
- **Entry signal** (a setting): Supertrend flips to the trade side; or price crosses the ATR trail while
  Supertrend agrees; or both agree for the first time. In every case the Kalman slope must point the same way.
- **Stop.** The last confirmed 15-minute swing low (for a long) or swing high (for a short).
- **Target.** 2R or 3R, where R is the distance from the decision close to the stop.
- **Volatility filter** (a setting): none; skip trades whose stop is wider than a multiple of ATR; skip when
  short-term ATR is high against long-term ATR; or require RVI on the trade side.
- **Exit** (a setting): fixed stop and target; the stop trails the ATR line; or close the trade when Supertrend
  flips against it.
- **Hours.** Four versions: all hours, or only inside the Asia, London or New York session (closed at the session
  end).

Same fixed properties as every test: $50,000, 1 contract, $2.01 commission per order, results at 0, 1 and 2 ticks
of slippage. [How the tester works](/method/).

## Search for a better filter (F2)

Before any search, I wrote down the rules a result must pass to count as a candidate. It had to make money at
1 **and** 2 ticks, at least two thirds of its nearby settings had to make money too, and no single year could
supply more than half of its profit. The main guess was the same as for Fair Price Reversal: trades with a small
stop lose the most once slippage is added.

The search used data before 2024 only and scored every setting on its profit at 1 tick.

<F2Runs strategy="supertrend_kalman" />

### What this means

The stop-size guess was right in its strongest form. In the all-hours version, skipping every trade with a stop
smaller than 100 ticks turned a loss at 2 ticks into a large profit. But more than half of that profit came from
2022 alone, so the rule written before the search rejects it. A stop of 100 ticks mostly happens when oil is very
volatile, so the filter is really a bet on volatile years.

The guided search found an even bigger all-hours number: +$90,347 at 2 ticks. It is the clearest failure of
all. 2022 alone made 93% of its profit, and half of the 14 years lost money. Six changed settings fitted one
year of crude oil.

New York is the only candidate, and it is a small one. The New York baseline already made money at 1 tick before
2024. The filter only raises its own RVI threshold from 50 to 55. Codex rebuilt it independently and every trade
matched. **None of this shows a tradable edge yet.**
