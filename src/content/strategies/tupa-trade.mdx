## The idea

This is how I trade by hand, written down so the tester can check it. It uses **no indicators**, only swing points:
the highs and lows where price turned.

Price always moves from a swing low to a swing high and back. So after a swing low, the next swing must be a swing
high, and it can only be one of two things: a **higher high (HH)** or a **lower high (LH)**. After a swing high, the
next swing is a **higher low (HL)** or a **lower low (LL)**. At every moment there are only two outcomes, and the
trade comes from reading which one price is choosing right now.

## Swing points

- A candle is a **swing high** when the candle before it is not higher (equal is allowed) and the candle after it
  is strictly lower. A swing low is the mirror. So a swing is only known one candle later.
- Equal counts as a break: a high equal to the last swing high is a HH, a low equal to the last swing low is a LL.
- Two swing highs with no swing low between them: only the higher one stays. Same for lows.
- A swing is **confirmed** one candle late, but it is only **real** once the opposite swing has formed after it.
  Until then it is a **candidate** and can still be replaced.

## Top-down reading

I read the same chart on six timeframes: daily, 4 hours, 1 hour, 15 minutes, 5 minutes and 1 minute. Only closed
candles count. The 4-hour candles start at 06:00 MYT during US summer or 07:00 MYT during US winter, like the CME trading day.

- **Downtrend** on a timeframe: its last higher low (HL) has been touched. **Uptrend:** its last lower high (LH)
  has been touched.
- The **tested timeframe** is the one with a candidate swing. For a short, that is a candidate swing low. The
  worst case for a short is that this low holds and price builds a swing high from it.
- **Short bias:** every timeframe below the tested one, down to 5 minutes, is already in a downtrend. Each lower
  timeframe that breaks down makes the worst case less likely.
- Longs are the exact mirror.

## The entry: a 1-minute structure break

With a short bias, I wait for the 1-minute chart to turn from up to down:

- The last 1-minute swing low is a higher low, and a swing high has formed after it.
- When price **touches** that higher low, structure has broken (a market structure shift). That level is the
  **break point**.
- The order is a **stop-limit**: it wakes up when price touches the break point, then sells at the limit price.

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Stop loss | Exactly at the last 1-minute swing high |
| Maximum risk | 25 ticks ($250 for one contract, before costs). If the stop is further away, the sell limit moves up toward it so the risk is exactly 25 ticks |
| Take profit | 25 ticks from the fill |
| Unfilled limit | Cancelled when the next 1-minute swing high forms |
| Session | New York only: 21:00–02:30 MYT during US summer, 22:00–03:30 MYT during US winter (ends the following day). One trade per session. An open trade is closed at the end |

## One example: 26 October 2026

The rules came from walking through real days with my charts. On 26 October:

- The 1-hour chart had a candidate higher low. The worst case for a short was that it would hold.
- The 15-minute and 5-minute charts had both already touched their last higher low, so both were in a downtrend.
- On the 1-minute chart, price touched the last higher low. The stop (the 1-minute swing high) was 26 ticks away,
  more than the 25-tick limit. So the sell limit sat 1 tick above the break point, which made the risk exactly 25
  ticks.
- A few minutes later price touched the break point and the order filled. The take profit was hit the next
  minute: +25 ticks, which is $250 minus $4.02 commission.

This is one hand-picked day that I used to write the rules, so it proves nothing about the method. The test had to
reproduce this trade before any of its other numbers were trusted, and it did: same minute, same prices, same
result, in my tester and in Codex's own code.

## How the test handles stop-limit orders

The tester had no stop-limit orders before this strategy. They now follow one rule. When price touches the trigger,
the order becomes a limit order. It can still fill later in the same candle if price comes back to the limit. A
limit fill uses the limit price or a better price. This rule has not been checked against TradingView yet. The first check is whether
TradingView takes the 26 October trade at the same minute and price.

### What this means

As coded, these rules have no edge on CL. The other three settings traded more often (2,000 to 4,100 trades) and
lost more ($32,852 to $34,158 after commission), so more trades did not help. Only 5 of the 17 years made money, none by
much.

These numbers test the rules as written down, not the trader. If my hand trading uses judgement the rules do not
capture yet, it is missing here. Before calling the method dead, the next step is to compare the coded trades with
how I would have traded the same days, and find the gap.

Codex rebuilt the 26 October trade and the whole F1 result with its own code and got the same numbers. Its review
also found a bug in the Pine version (it stopped trading after the first trade), now fixed. It does not prove
TradingView gives the same numbers: only a TradingView export of the Pine version can do that.
