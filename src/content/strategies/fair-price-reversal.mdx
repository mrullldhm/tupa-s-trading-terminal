## The idea

The original indicator by LuxAlgo watches one candle: the last 1-minute candle before the US stock market opens at
9:30 ET. Its body is treated as a **fair price**. When the open pushes price far away from that level and then the
move runs out of steam, price often snaps back toward it.

The original was made for Nasdaq futures. Here the same idea runs on **CL1!**, front-month crude oil, at each of the
three CL session opens instead of 9:30 ET. Click through the steps below to see one trade from start to finish.

## The rules

The signal is the indicator's own logic, unchanged. The trade rules are mine, because the indicator only draws
signals and never trades.

- **Fair price.** The body (open to close) of the candle just before the session opens.
- **Setup window.** Starts at the session open. Price must move beyond the body by at least a set multiple of
  ATR(14). ATR is a measure of normal candle size.
- **Long signal.** After a move down, a lower swing high forms. A candle then closes above that swing high while
  still below the body. Shorts are the mirror image. At most one long and one short per session per day.
- **Entry.** A market order at the next candle's open, only when no trade is open.
- **Stop.** The most extreme price of the move away, fixed when the signal fires.
- **Target.** One of four choices, tested as a setting: the near body edge, the far body edge, 1R or 2R. R is
  the distance from the signal close to the stop.
- **Session end.** Any open trade is closed at the flat time: the close order is sent on the candle before, so it
  fills at the flat minute's open (or the next open if that candle is missing).

Every test uses the same fixed properties: $50,000 starting capital, 1 contract, $2.01 commission per order
($4.02 for a round trip). Results are shown at 0 ticks of slippage and again at 1 and 2 ticks. One tick on CL is
$10 per contract. [How the tester works](/method/) explains every fill rule.

## Feature 1: test every setting

Feature 1 runs every combination of four settings (192 in total) for each session over 16 years of 1-minute data,
then reports the one with the highest net profit at 0 ticks. Below is that winner, rerun at 1 and 2 ticks.

## Feature 2: search for a better filter

Feature 1 showed the problem is costs. So Feature 2 asked a narrow question, written down **before** the search:
do trades with a small stop lose the most once slippage is added? One tick on entry and one on exit is $20, which
eats a large share of a small stop but a small share of a big one.

The search only used data before 2024. It optimized net profit **at 1 tick**, so it could not reward edges smaller
than slippage. Every run is listed below, including the ones that failed.

### What this means

The filter that won in New York and London is the one the hypothesis predicted: skip trades whose stop is
smaller than about 30 ticks. Its neighbours also work, so it is not a lucky spike. But it has only been tested on
the same data it was picked on, and most of London's profit came from one volatile year (2022). A stop size in
ticks also changes meaning when the oil price changes. **None of this shows a tradable edge yet.**
